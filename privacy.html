Privacy Policy

This covers two different things: this marketing website, and the TuneZinc app itself. They handle data very differently, so we've kept them separate below.

Last updated: August 11, 2026

Overview

TuneZinc the app is local-first: it's designed to run entirely on your own devices, with your music library stored as real files, not in a cloud account. TuneZinc the website (this site, hosted on GitHub Pages) is a simple marketing page with no login and no tracking scripts. This policy explains what little data either one touches, and where a small number of third-party lookups fit in.

This website

tunezinc.com is a static site hosted on GitHub Pages. We don't run analytics, advertising, or tracking scripts, and this site doesn't set any cookies of its own.

Because the site is served by GitHub's infrastructure, GitHub itself processes standard web request data (like IP address, browser type, and request timestamps) to serve pages and keep the service secure, per GitHub's Privacy Statement. We don't have access to that data ourselves beyond aggregate, anonymized traffic stats GitHub may make available to repository owners.

If you contact us by email, we'll use your email address only to reply to you.

The TuneZinc app

No account, no cloud backend

TuneZinc doesn't require sign-up or a user account, and there's no TuneZinc server that your library, listening activity, or personal data is sent to. The app is built to work fully offline.

What stays on your device

Everything the app needs to function is stored locally, in app storage on the device you install it on:

  • Your music files and the folders you point the app at
  • Track metadata, embedded tags, and any edits you make to them
  • Playlists, the play queue, and playback state (e.g. volume, position)
  • Listening stats — play counts, skips, and thumbs up/down ratings
  • The on-device recommendation index used for "song radio," auto-mix, and playlist suggestions

None of this is uploaded anywhere by default. It never leaves your device unless a feature described below sends a specific, limited piece of it out — and each of those is opt-in or clearly part of a feature you're actively using.

Third-party lookups the app makes

A few features work by querying external, independent services over the internet. These are the only cases where data leaves your device to a third party:

  • MusicBrainz — used for metadata cleanup, album art, and fixing "Unknown Artist/Album" tracks, and to power external search results. Lookups send search terms (like artist, album, or track title) and/or acoustic identifiers to MusicBrainz's servers, operated by the MetaBrainz Foundation. See the MetaBrainz Privacy Policy.
  • LRCLIB — used to auto-fetch synced lyrics when they aren't already embedded in a file or provided as a local .lrc file. Lookups send track/artist/duration info to LRCLIB to find a matching lyrics file.

These lookups only include the metadata needed to find a match (e.g. song title, artist, duration) — not your listening history, ratings, or library as a whole. Neither service is used for analytics or advertising by TuneZinc, and we don't control how these independent, third-party services handle the requests they receive — see their own policies linked above.

Syncing your own devices ("Mesh")

TuneZinc can sync your library and let you remote-control playback between your own devices — e.g. phone and desktop — over your local network. This is peer-to-peer between devices you pair yourself; no TuneZinc server or third party is involved, and it only works when devices are on the same network and discoverable to each other.

Pairing requires a password you set, but sync and remote-control traffic itself is not encrypted — it's designed for trusted home networks, not the open internet. Don't use Mesh sync on networks you don't trust.

Add-ons

TuneZinc supports optional, user-installed add-ons (scripts) that can act on your tracks, albums, playlists, or artists. Add-ons run locally and only with your explicit consent before each run, and the app shows live console output while one is running. Because an add-on is an external script, what it does with any data it can access is up to that add-on's author, not TuneZinc — only install add-ons from sources you trust.

Permissions the app may request

Depending on platform, TuneZinc may ask for permissions like file/storage access (to scan your music library) or local network access (for Mesh sync and remote control). These are used only for the features described above.

Children's privacy

TuneZinc isn't directed at children, and given the app collects no account or personal data by design, there's nothing distinct to collect from a child user versus anyone else.
